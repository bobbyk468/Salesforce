The Salesforce Architect track is the most expensive and most misunderstood
part of the certification catalogue. Seventeen credentials, $12,900 in exam
fees if you sat every one, and a structure that is not obvious from the
outside: two of the seventeen cannot be sat at all.

Here is what the track actually looks like in 2026, with the real figures.

## The two you cannot sit

[Application Architect](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/application-architect)
and [System Architect](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/system-architect)
have no exam of their own. They are awarded for holding a set of component
credentials — which is why their prerequisite reads "see component exams" and
why their duration, question count and pass mark are all blank in any honest
dataset.

This catches people out constantly. You do not book System Architect. You
earn it, and the $400 next to it is the aggregate of what you paid for the
components, not a separate fee.

Broadly:

- **Application Architect** comes from the application-side credentials —
  Platform Data Architect, Platform Sharing and Visibility Architect, Platform
  App Builder and Platform Developer I.
- **System Architect** comes from the system-side ones — Platform Integration
  Architect, Platform Identity and Access Management Architect, and Platform
  Development Lifecycle and Deployment Architect.

Check the current component list on Salesforce's own page before you plan
around it; the composition has changed before.

## The CTA, and why the track total is misleading

$12,900 for 17 credentials makes the Architect track look five times the price
of any other. It mostly is not. Three of those seventeen are the Certified
Technical Architect components:

| Stage | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| [Technical Architect (CTA)](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/technical-architect) | $1,500 |
| [Architect Evaluation](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/technical-architect-evaluation) | $1,500 |
| [Architect Review Board](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/technical-architect-review-board) | $4,500 |

That is **$7,500 — well over half the track** — concentrated in one credential
that a few hundred people worldwide hold. The Review Board is not a
multiple-choice exam; it is a live board session judged by sitting CTAs, which
is why there is no question count or pass mark to publish for it.

Strip the CTA out and the Architect track is $5,400 for fourteen credentials.
That is an ordinary number, and it is the one to budget against unless the
Review Board is genuinely your plan.

## A workable order

1. **Get the platform fundamentals out of the way first.** Platform App
   Builder and Platform Developer I are prerequisites in practice even where
   they are only "recommended".
2. **Pick a side.** Application-side if your work is data models, sharing and
   declarative design; system-side if it is integration, identity and release
   management. Do not alternate — the component credentials reinforce each
   other within a side.
3. **Sit the low-pass-mark exams first for momentum**, not because they are
   easier. Platform Data Architect and Platform Sharing and Visibility
   Architect both pass at 58%, the lowest in the catalogue.
4. **Treat Integration Architect as the hinge.** Its 67% pass mark and the
   breadth of its scope make it the one most people underestimate.
5. **Only then think about the CTA**, and only with employer backing. $7,500
   and a board review is not a self-funded weekend project.

## Two credentials to check before planning

[B2B Solution Architect](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/b2b-solution-architect)
and [B2C Commerce Architect](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/b2c-commerce-architect)
are both closed to new registrations. They still appear in listings, and
people still plan tracks around them. Check status, not just fee — several of
the cheapest-looking credentials in the catalogue are cheap because they are
on the way out.

Across the whole catalogue, 25 of the 87 current Salesforce credentials have a
