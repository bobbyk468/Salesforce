These three certifications cost the same ($200), run the same length (105
minutes), and ask the same number of scored questions (60). On paper they look
interchangeable. They are not — the pass marks differ by five points and the
section weightings describe three genuinely different jobs.

## The headline numbers

| | [Platform Administrator](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/administrator) | [Platform App Builder](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/app-builder) | [Business Analyst](https://www.trailblazeprep.com/certifications/business-analyst) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exam fee | $200 | $200 | $200 |
| Duration | 105 min | 105 min | 105 min |
| Scored questions | 60 | 60 | 60 |
| **Pass mark** | **68%** | **73%** | **72%** |
| Recommended first | Platform Foundations | Platform Foundations | Platform Administrator |

The pass mark is the figure to notice. App Builder needs 73% — the highest of
the three, and five points above ADM-201. On a 60-question exam, five
percentage points is three questions. That is not a rounding difference; it is
the margin most people fail by.

## What each exam actually tests

This is where they stop looking alike.

**Platform Administrator (ADM-201)** spreads across eight sections, and the
spread is the point — it is a breadth exam:

| Section | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Configuration and Setup | 20% |
| Object Manager and Lightning App Builder | 17% |
| Data and Analytics Management | 17% |
| Workflow and Process Automation | 14% |
| Sales and Marketing Applications | 11% |
| Service and Support Applications | 9% |
| Agentforce (new in Summer '26) | 8% |
| Productivity and Collaboration | 4% |

Note the Agentforce section at 8%. That is new in Summer '26 and it is the
thing most out-of-date study material misses entirely.

**Platform App Builder** is far more concentrated:

| Section | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Business Logic and Process Automation | 32% |
| Data Modeling and Management | 20% |
| Salesforce Fundamentals | 18% |
| User Interface | 17% |
| App Deployment | 13% |

Nearly a third of the exam is one section. Business Logic and Process
Automation alone is worth more than ADM-201's bottom four sections combined.
If flows, validation rules and the automation decision matrix are not solid,
no amount of breadth saves you — and with a 73% pass mark there is very little
room.

**Business Analyst** is not a platform exam at all:

| Section | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Collaboration with Stakeholders | 23% |
| Requirements | 18% |
| User Stories | 18% |
| Customer Discovery | 17% |
| Business Process Mapping | 12% |
| Development Support and User Acceptance | 12% |

There is no "Configuration and Setup" here. More than half the exam is
discovery, stakeholders and requirements. People who are strong admins and
assume this is an easy follow-on fail it regularly, because it tests a
different skill that happens to be assessed on the same platform.

## So which one first?

**Start with ADM-201** if you are new. It is the lowest pass mark of the
three, it is the credential employers actually screen for, and its breadth
gives you the vocabulary the other two assume. It is also the prerequisite
Salesforce recommends before Business Analyst.

**Go App Builder next** if your work is building — objects, flows, Lightning
pages. The overlap with ADM-201 is real but narrower than people expect:
ADM-201's Object Manager and Lightning App Builder section is 17%, while App
Builder's equivalent ground is more than half the paper and tested deeper.
Budget separate study time, and respect the 73%.

**Go Business Analyst next** if your work is closer to the business than the
org — requirements, process mapping, running workshops. Do not go there
expecting a victory lap after ADM-201. Different exam, different discipline,
72% to pass.

## The trap in all three

All three report your result as a percentage per section with no overall
figure, so a fail tells you which sections were weak but not how close you
were. Because the sections are weighted so unevenly — 32% versus 13% on App
Builder — the section worth fixing is rarely your lowest score. Work it out
