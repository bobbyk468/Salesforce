If you have a Salesforce certification on your CV and have not looked at the
catalogue lately, there is a reasonable chance it no longer exists under that
name. **34 credentials have been renamed**, and a further **25 have a changed
exam status** — retired, or closed to new registrations.

Nothing was taken away from anyone. Your credential is still yours. But the
name on your CV, the name in the job advert, and the name on the Salesforce
page are now three different strings, and search engines treat them as three
different things. That is a practical problem for anyone hiring, anyone
applying, and anyone trying to find study material.

## The renames that catch people out

A few patterns account for most of them.

**"Administrator" became "Platform Administrator."** ADM-201 is now Platform
Administrator; Advanced Administrator is Platform Administrator II. The exam
codes people actually use in conversation — ADM-201, ADM-211 — were never
official names in the first place, which is why they have outlived two
renamings.

**Consultants became Agentforce Consultants.** Sales Cloud Consultant is
Agentforce Sales Consultant. Service Cloud Consultant is Agentforce Service
Consultant. Field Service Consultant is Agentforce Field Service *and
Operations* Consultant. Nonprofit Cloud Consultant is Agentforce Nonprofit
Consultant. Same exams, new front name tracking a product strategy — and note
that two of those gained words in the middle, not just a prefix, so a
find-and-replace over your records will miss them.

**Pardot became Marketing Cloud Account Engagement.** This one is years old
and still catching people, because "Pardot" is what the whole ecosystem calls
it and what everyone searches for.

**Data Cloud became Data 360.** Recent, and the most confusing, because
"Data Cloud" is still in wide use in job adverts.

**Architect credentials gained a "Platform" prefix.** Data Architect is
Platform Data Architect; Integration Architect is Platform Integration
Architect; Sharing and Visibility, Identity and Access Management, and Dev
Lifecycle and Deployment all took the same prefix. Platform Developer I lost
its numeral and is simply Platform Developer.

**Nonprofit Success Pack (NPSP) Consultant is closed** to new registrations,
which is a status change rather than a rename — a separate column, and a
separate problem.

## Why the former names still matter

Search volume does not follow a rename. People search for what they called it
when they learned it. If you are writing about Salesforce certifications,
keeping the former name visible — "Agentforce Sales Consultant (formerly
Sales Cloud Consultant)" — is not clutter, it is the only way the person
looking for it finds you. If you are applying for a job, put both.

That is why the rename tracker is a first-class table in the dataset rather
than a footnote: for 34 credentials, the former name is the primary search
term and the current name is the thing you have to sit.

## One warning about retirements

"Closed to new registrations" and "retired" are not the same thing, and the
dataset distinguishes them. A closed exam means you can no longer book it; it
does not mean existing holders lose anything, and it does not always mean a
replacement exists yet. Before you plan a track around a credential, check
its status, not just its fee — several of the cheapest credentials in the
catalogue are cheap because they are on the way out.
