---

[Tweet 2]
when a hiring manager asks "walk me through the sharing model" — they already know the answer.

they're checking that you do too. that's the filter.

pass it and you hit the real evaluation: can you think through ambiguous problems with stakeholders breathing down your neck?

---

[Tweet 3]
the questions that decide things:

"tell me about a time a stakeholder pushed back on your solution."
"walk me through a project that didn't go as planned."

no right answer. they're watching how you think, not what you know.

---

[Tweet 4]
"declarative vs code" question trips people up because nobody teaches it.

salesforce answer: flow if flow can. code only when it genuinely can't.
the follow-up they'll ask: "give me an example where you had to use code anyway."
have that story ready before you walk in.

---

[Tweet 5]
i've watched phd-level admins get rejected because they argued with a vp instead of shipping the half-assed deadline version.

'i explained best practice and they agreed' is a lie. they want to know you can compromise without breaking the org.

---

[Tweet 6]
the gap between candidates who get offers and those who don't: specificity.

"i've worked on automation" loses to "i built a flow that cut manual assignment time from 4 hours to 20 minutes."

every time. numbers and before/after beat vague experience.

---

[Tweet 7]
no real project experience?

trailhead projects, nonprofits, personal dev orgs — all count.

"i completed the superbadge" is not a story. "i built X that solved Y for Z" is.

one good specific project beats a resume full of certifications.

if you're taking the exam, forget the keynote magic. it tests three things:

topics — what the agent handles
actions — what it can do within a topic
instructions — how it decides when to act

agentforce will purposefully block itself.

"agent routes billing questions to sales instead of support. what's wrong?"

ID: month2-thread-study-method
Post: Monday April 28

[Tweet 1 — hook]
i've taken 14 salesforce certification exams. failed two. changed my study method five times.

the method that finally worked is embarrassingly simple.

most people aren't using it. here's what it is. 🧵

#SalesforceCertification #SalesforceAdmin

---

[Tweet 2]
before you open a single course: download the official exam guide from trailhead.

it lists every topic and the exact percentage each one is worth. if you study without this document you're just guessing.

---

[Tweet 3]
before you've studied anything: take a practice exam.

not to pass. to find out where you're actually weak.

a 40% score with topic breakdown is worth more than 6 weeks of unfocused studying. it tells you exactly what to fix. most people save this for the end. backwards.

---

[Tweet 4]
your entire study list comes from exactly two places.

the exam guide tells you what's tested and the weight. your practice exam shows exactly where you're bleeding points.

everything else is just noise.

---

[Tweet 5]
study in blocks. one topic. 45 minutes. finish it before you stop.

security model. automation. data management. one at a time.

trying to learn sharing rules while reviewing flow limits means you'll fail questions on both. context switching destroys retention.

---

[Tweet 6]
every time you read something, immediately go do it.

read about sharing rules? go configure one. read about omnichannel? build a basic setup.

the exam gives you a scenario. your brain needs to know what to do with it — not just what it is.

---

[Tweet 7]
two weeks before the exam: stop learning new things.

only review what you've already covered.

new material at that stage creates doubt more than it creates readiness. your only job in the final stretch is to sharpen what you have.

---

take the free practice test that actually shows your gaps before you waste another $200:
